# Load limits and bucket loading

Every machine has a rated capacity. Working above it does not make the job faster. It wears the bucket, pins, and hydraulics, raises the risk of tipping, and often slows the cycle because the machine struggles to lift and swing.

## What the payload reading means

SAFE2GO shows payload as a percentage of the machine's rated capacity. A full, well-shaped bucket is usually between 85% and 100%.

- Above 105%: WARNING. The load is heavier than the machine is rated for.
- Above 120%: CRITICAL. The machine may become unstable, especially when swinging or on uneven ground.
- Three or more overload incidents in one shift are recorded as a pattern for coaching.

## Why buckets get overloaded

- Dense material. Wet clay and rock weigh much more per bucket than dry topsoil. The same bucket fill can be a normal load in one material and an overload in another.
- Heaping the bucket to save a cycle.
- Digging too deep in one pass and curling a full face of material.
- Water in the material after rain.

## How to load within limits

1. Adjust the fill to the material. Take a smaller bite in wet clay, rock, or saturated soil.
2. Aim for a smooth, level bucket rather than a heaped one.
3. Keep the load low and close to the machine while swinging or travelling.
4. Swing slowly with a heavy load. Stop the swing gently to avoid shifting the load.
5. When loading trucks, spread the load evenly and do not exceed the truck's own limit.

## Stability and load together

A heavy load raised high moves the combined centre of gravity up and out. On a slope, or when swinging to the side, this can tip the machine even below its rated capacity. Keep heavy loads low, work uphill where possible, and avoid swinging a full bucket to the downhill side.

## When an overload alert appears

Lower the load to a safe height, reduce the bucket fill, and continue. A single warning is common while learning a new material. Repeated warnings are a sign to change technique rather than push harder.
