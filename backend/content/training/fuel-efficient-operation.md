# Fuel-efficient operation

Fuel is one of the largest running costs of a machine. A large share of it is burned while the machine is not doing useful work. Small habits in the cab make a measurable difference over a shift.

## Idling

An idling excavator or loader still burns fuel, adds engine hours, and brings service intervals closer. It also adds noise and exhaust for people working nearby.

- If you expect to wait more than a few minutes, shut the engine down.
- Use the auto-idle and automatic engine shutdown features where the machine has them.
- Plan with the site team so trucks arrive when you are ready, not the other way round.

SAFE2GO records an idling pattern when the engine runs for 5 minutes or more with no hydraulic activity and no travel. Time marked as blocked, for example waiting for a truck, is not counted. If you are waiting for a reason outside your control, mark the task as blocked so the record is fair.

## Idle ratio

The idle ratio is the share of engine-on time spent idling. SAFE2GO compares your shift to your own typical range. A shift well above that range is noted for coaching. It is not a penalty. It often points to a planning issue, such as waiting for trucks or unclear task order.

## Engine speed and travel

Running at high engine speed while travelling burns fuel quickly without adding productive work.

- Use the lowest engine speed and work mode that gets the job done. Most machines have an economy mode for normal digging and loading.
- Reduce engine speed when travelling. Save high power for heavy digging and lifting.
- Plan routes to keep travel distances short.

## Efficient cycles

1. Position trucks so the swing angle is small, ideally 45 to 90 degrees.
2. Dig in a smooth sequence rather than chasing material around the work face.
3. Avoid spinning wheels on loaders. It burns fuel and wears tyres.
4. Keep the bucket teeth and cutting edges in good condition. Worn teeth make every cycle harder.

## Daily checks that save fuel

- Check air filter indicators. A blocked filter increases fuel use.
- Check tyre pressures on wheeled machines.
- Report hydraulic leaks. Leaks waste oil and power.
